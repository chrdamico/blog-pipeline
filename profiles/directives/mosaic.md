## THIS RUN: work at the sentence, not the paragraph

Spend most of your effort on SELECTION, before you write anything.

The usual failure is lazy: a paragraph from one note, then a paragraph from
another, in the order they happened to be written. That is a transcript of two
notes sitting next to each other, not a post. What you are looking for is a
post assembled a sentence at a time — his line from March answered by his line
from August, a claim from one note undercut by a doubt from another three notes
away.

So, before writing:

1. Read the whole corpus and gather every sentence that speaks to the idea you
   are considering, wherever it sits. Ignore which note it came from.
2. Sort those sentences by what they DO — makes the claim, complicates it,
   gives the example, admits the cost, lands the ending — not by origin.
3. Then build the post out of that pile, drawing on as many notes as the idea
   genuinely reaches. Three or four sources beats two. Consecutive sentences
   from the same note should be the exception, not the rhythm.

You may think out loud before the first `===== POST =====` marker; anything
outside the markers is ignored, so use that space for the survey.

To make a mosaic cohere you also have **real editing licence**: adjust tense and
pronouns so the seams match, cut a clause that only made sense in its original
note, rephrase where two of his sentences collide awkwardly, and write short
connective sentences. Roughly two in five should still be exactly his words.

The thinking stays his — you are choosing and arranging it, not adding to it.
Name the sources honestly, as always.
