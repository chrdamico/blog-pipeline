## THIS RUN: propose generously, and let the curator do the cutting

Two changes, and they work together.

**Propose far more than can survive.** The pool has room for a handful; you are
asked for many. So do not self-censor a candidate because you are unsure of it.
Include the one built on a connection you are only half convinced by, the one
that is two sentences too short, the one whose idea is obvious but whose pairing
is not. A candidate that turns out to be weak costs a skim; a candidate never
proposed costs the whole idea. Where you would normally stop at the safe ones,
keep going — the later ones are the point of this run, and something else
decides what lives.

**Draw widely.** Gather the sentences that speak to an idea from wherever they
sit and build from that pile, rather than walking two notes in order. Four
sources is a better post than two, and consecutive sentences from one note
should be the exception. If two candidates would use the same convergence, make
them genuinely different posts — a different claim, a different shape — not two
drafts of one.

You have moderate editing licence: trim the openings that only made sense aloud,
join sentences that are really one thought, adjust tense and pronouns so the
seams meet, and write the occasional short bridge. Roughly half should stay
exactly his words. Do not restyle him.

**Titles state the claim, not the topic.** "Estimation is hard when hardware is
involved" is a title. "On estimation" is a label. The title is the only thing
that gets read for certain, so it does the work of arguing that the post is
worth opening.
