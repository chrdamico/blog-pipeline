## This run's directive

Cut the hedge the author starts sentences with.

Dictating produces a verbal run-up: before the actual claim arrives, a sentence
opens with a phrase that carries no information and only fills the moment while
the thought assembles. **"I think" is the one to watch.** He begins something
like one sentence in five with it, and almost never because he is marking a
sentence as uncertain — he is clearing his throat. On paper it reads as
apologising for every statement he makes.

So: when a sentence begins with such a phrase and the sentence means the same
thing without it, delete the phrase and let the claim open the sentence.

  in:  "I think the problem is that nobody wrote it down."
  out: "The problem is that nobody wrote it down."

  in:  "I mean, I guess it's mostly a scheduling thing."
  out: "It's mostly a scheduling thing."

The same family, when it is doing the same nothing: "I guess", "I mean",
"I feel like", "I believe", "I would say", "for me", "somehow", "actually",
"basically", "kind of", "sort of", "genau", "eigentlich", "halt", "irgendwie".
And in the middle of a sentence too, where these pad rather than qualify.

KEEP IT when the uncertainty is the content, not the run-up. He is entitled to
hedge on purpose, and stripping a deliberate hedge turns a guess into a claim he
never made — which is exactly the failure this pipeline exists to prevent. Keep
it when:

- he contrasts what he thinks with what is true, or with what somebody else
  thinks ("I think it was Tuesday, but I'd have to check");
- the sentence is visibly an act of weighing, guessing or predicting, and
  without the hedge it would read as a settled fact;
- the hedge is the joke, the rhythm, or the turn in the thinking — a "I think"
  that lands after two sentences of certainty is doing work;
- removing it would leave you unable to tell whether he is reporting or
  speculating.

When in doubt, keep it. A surviving filler costs a slightly limp sentence; a
wrongly-cut hedge costs him a position he does not hold, published verbatim in
his own name.

Two mechanical rules. First, what is left has to be a whole sentence, correctly
capitalised — supply nothing but the capital and, where the cut orphaned it, the
subject the phrase was carrying. Second, the hedge either goes or stays where it
is: never relocate it (no "…, I think." appended to the end) and never replace
it with a different opener, a connective, or an adverb of your own. The point is
that the sentence starts sooner, not that it starts differently.

Expect many sentences to now begin bluntly with their subject, several in a row.
That is the intended sound and not a problem to fix. Do not vary the openings.

Everything else in the mandate above still holds — no dropped clause, no dropped
sentence, no dropped paragraph, no reordering, no restyling, no smoothing of the
Denglisch. This directive licenses removing one class of contentless opener and
nothing more.

(This is an experiment: `changes.diff` and `churn_pct` in the bundle's
`meta.json` say afterwards how much it actually moved, and the count of
sentence-initial "I think" in `cleaned.md` says whether it worked at all.)
