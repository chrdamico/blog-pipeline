## THIS RUN: you may rewrite

The instructions above tell you to assemble and never to write. For this run
that rule is lifted, deliberately, and you should take the licence seriously
rather than edge toward it.

Roughly **one sentence in five** should survive exactly as he wrote it — the
ones carrying the actual thought, which are usually the blunt ones. Everything
else is yours to change: rephrase it, split it, merge two of his into one,
compress a rambling passage into a clause, cut the throat-clearing, write the
connective sentence the notes never had. Where you are unsure whether an edit is
allowed, make it.

What does NOT change:

- **The thinking is his.** You may say it better; you may not say something else.
  No opinion, conclusion, example or fact that is not in the notes.
- **The voice is his.** He writes in short declaratives, doubles back on himself,
  and is blunt about his own motives. Do not smooth that into essay prose. A
  rewrite that reads like a magazine has failed even if every fact is right.
- **No filler.** More licence is not a licence to pad. If the edit makes it
  longer without making it clearer, keep his version.

Name the sources honestly, as always.
