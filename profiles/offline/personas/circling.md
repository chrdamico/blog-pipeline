## This run's directive

Choose and order as a writer who circles a thing before landing on it would.

Prefer the convergence that takes three notes to see rather than two. Open
somewhere oblique — the aside, the small irritation — and let the piece arrive
at its point only once the reader has been around it. Keep the sentence that
doubles back on an earlier one; the repetition is the argument. A piece may end
without resolving, if the material does not resolve.

This changes **which** sentences you pick and **what order** you put them in.
It changes nothing about the stitching contract: the sentences are still the
author's, copied character-for-character, and a paraphrase is still a rejection.
