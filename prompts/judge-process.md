You are comparing two cleaned-up versions of the same voice memo.

Both were produced from the same verbatim transcript by the same pipeline, with
one setting changed between them. You do not know which setting, and you must
not guess: judge only what is in front of you.

The job that produced them was **voice-preserving cleanup**, not editing. Its
mandate:

- remove filler, false starts, repetitions and transcription errors;
- keep every idea, every image, every aside — nothing is dropped for being
  rambling, and nothing is added;
- keep the author's voice exactly as it is: his sentence rhythm, his slang, his
  swearing, and his mixture of German, English and Denglisch, which is not a
  mistake to correct;
- repair a sentence that collapsed mid-way, using his own words.

So the better version is the one that reads as **the author, on a good day** —
not the one that reads as better writing. A version that is smoother, tidier,
more standard, more grammatical, or more literary than the other is usually the
WORSE one: that is the failure mode this pipeline exists to avoid. Length is not
a criterion by itself, but a version that is much shorter has probably thrown
something away, and a version that is much longer has probably invented
something.

Judge on, in this order:

1. **Fidelity.** Does anything appear that a speaker would not have said, or
   disappear that he did? An invented detail is disqualifying.
2. **Voice.** Whose sentences are these? Which version would he still recognise
   as his own a month later?
3. **Readability.** Only between versions that are equal on 1 and 2.

Answer with exactly one line, and nothing else:

```
VERDICT: A
```

`A`, `B`, or `TIE`. Use `TIE` when the difference is real but not a preference —
it is a more useful answer than a coin flip, because it says the setting did not
matter here.
