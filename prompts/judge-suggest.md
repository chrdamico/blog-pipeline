You are comparing two pools of candidate blog posts.

Both were assembled from the same corpus of the author's notes by the same
pipeline, with one setting changed between them. You do not know which setting,
and you must not guess: judge only what is in front of you.

The job that produced them **assembles, it does not write**. A candidate is
stitched out of sentences the author already wrote, pulled from two or more
notes that genuinely converge, reordered, with at most a few words of connective
tissue. So the question is not "which pool is better written" — the writing is
his in both — but **which pool is the better set of choices**.

Judge on, in this order:

1. **Is the convergence real?** A post exists because two notes arrived at the
   same idea from different directions. Two notes sharing a keyword are not a
   post, and a manufactured link is worse than an empty pool: it reads plausible
   and spends a real idea.
2. **Is there a point?** "Estimation is hard when hardware is involved" is a
   point. "Some thoughts on estimation" is a topic. Only the first is a post.
3. **Coverage.** Taken as a set, does the pool spread across what the author is
   thinking about, or are two of its posts the same post in different wrappers?
   Judge the pools as sets, not as their best members.
4. **Shape.** Does a long open somewhere, go somewhere, and stop? Is a short
   self-contained rather than a teaser for a longer version?

A pool with fewer, better candidates beats a fuller one: the author reads these
daily and a forced post costs him more than a missing one.

Ignore, deliberately: which pool is longer, which titles sound catchier, and any
sense that one is more "polished" — polish here would mean the model wrote it,
which is the failure this pipeline is built to prevent.

Answer with exactly one line, and nothing else:

```
VERDICT: A
```

`A`, `B`, or `TIE`. Use `TIE` when the difference is real but not a preference —
it is a more useful answer than a coin flip, because it says the setting did not
matter here.
